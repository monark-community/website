## Start with the Basics

Before you write any code, get comfortable with the fundamentals:

- Blockchain basics: distributed ledgers, consensus mechanisms (Proof of Work, Proof of Stake) and the trade-offs between scalability, security and decentralization.
- Cryptography: public and private key pairs, hashing, digital signatures and zero-knowledge proofs, which underpin security and privacy in Web3.
- Decentralization beyond the technology: governance, tokenomics (how a token's supply and rewards are designed) and community incentives.
- Ethereum and the alternatives: Ethereum is still the main platform for smart contracts, but it's worth knowing Solana, Polkadot and layer 2 solutions.

Two good reads to start: [Mastering Bitcoin by Andreas M. Antonopoulos](https://github.com/bitcoinbook/bitcoinbook) and the [Ethereum Whitepaper](https://ethereum.org/whitepaper/).

## Smart Contracts and Tools

Smart contracts are the programs behind decentralized applications. Solidity is the language for Ethereum; Rust (for Solana) and Vyper are other options. Tools and resources:

- [Updraft](https://updraft.cyfrin.io/), an interactive Solidity learning platform supported by Monark
- [Hardhat](https://hardhat.org/) and [Truffle](https://www.trufflesuite.com/), frameworks for developing and testing on Ethereum
- OpenZeppelin, a library of audited, reusable smart contracts
- [CryptoZombies](https://cryptozombies.io/), Solidity tutorials in the form of a game
- the [Solidity Docs](https://docs.soliditylang.org/en/v0.8.30/)

A deployed smart contract can't be changed, so thorough testing and security audits are a must.

## The Rest of the Stack

A Web3 application needs more than smart contracts:

- Indexing and queries: [The Graph](https://thegraph.com/) provides decentralized data querying, so apps can read blockchain data quickly and reliably. Monark hopes to partner with The Graph to bring its indexing to our projects.
- Infrastructure: [Pinax](https://pinax.network/) offers decentralized infrastructure services that help developers build resilient apps.
- Zero-knowledge technology: [Midnight Network](https://midnightnetwork.io/) specializes in zero-knowledge proofs and privacy-preserving tools, which fits Monark's goal of secure, private Web3 systems.
- Storage: [IPFS](https://docs.filecoin.io/basics/how-storage-works/filecoin-and-ipfs) and [Arweave](https://arweave.org/) are decentralized storage protocols that keep content available over time.
- Wallets: [MetaMask](https://metamask.io/) and [WalletConnect](https://walletconnect.network/) handle sign-in and transaction signing.
- Oracles: [Chainlink](https://chain.link/) and [Band Protocol](https://www.bandprotocol.com/) connect smart contracts to real-world data.
- Scaling: [Optimism](https://www.optimism.io/), [zkSync](https://www.zksync.io/) and [Polygon](https://polygon.technology/) are layer 2 solutions that improve performance.
- Front end: [Viem + Wagmi](https://viem.sh/), and tools such as The Graph's subgraphs.

## Keeping Up

Web3 changes fast. Newsletters such as [Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/newsletters) and [Week in Ethereum](https://weekinethereumnews.com/) help, and so do developer communities on Discord and forums, and regular hackathons and workshops.

Web3 development is demanding and keeps changing, but with solid foundations, the right tools and good infrastructure partners, it's within reach and rewarding. Monark collaborates with The Graph, Pinax Network and Midnight Network to support the developers building on it.

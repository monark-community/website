## What the early internet teaches us

The internet didn't take off overnight. Early users put up with slow connections, clunky interfaces and technical jargon. It took years for broadband to spread and for easy-to-use browsers to appear, and for the internet to go from a tool for "nerds" to something useful to everyone.

Web3 is at a similar turning point. The technology exists, but complexity and friction keep adoption low.

## What still needs work

- Wallets. Managing private keys, seed phrases and transaction fees is confusing and risky for non-experts. Wallets need to be as simple and secure as a regular login, without giving up decentralization.
- Identity. Web3 identity is tied to wallets, which feel impersonal and are hard to relate to. People need identity systems that are more intuitive and protect their privacy.
- Regulation. Clearer legal frameworks would reduce uncertainty and protect users without stifling innovation.
- Education. People need to understand Web3's benefits and risks. Without that, misinformation and scams will keep newcomers away.

## Web2 and Web3 together

For many users, Web3 won't replace Web2 overnight. Hybrid approaches can combine the strengths of both: familiar Web2 interfaces on top of decentralized back ends, Web2-style onboarding that eases people into Web3 features, and work with existing institutions to build trust and legitimacy.

## A long road

Web3's promise of user ownership, transparency and decentralization is compelling, but it isn't ready for everyday use yet. Getting there takes patient, honest work on usability, education, infrastructure and regulation.

At Monark, we're working toward that by bringing academia, industry and the community together to build the tools and frameworks that will make Web3 accessible to everyone. It's a marathon, and we think the destination is worth it.
